# Marketing and Editorial Surfaces

## When to use

Use for landing pages, reports, proposals, briefs, pricing, case studies, benchmarks, blogs, changelogs, documentation, and narrative product pages.

## Inputs

- audience, context, decision, strongest supported answer, evidence, and caveat;
- supplied claims, customer identity, approvals, privacy, dates, and calls to action;
- brand shell, typography, assets, tone, and existing site structure;
- content source order, exact terminology, and audit material.

## Editorial procedure

1. **Frame the reader.** Identify who opens the page, what they need to understand or decide, and what evidence earns the answer.
2. **Normalize facts.** Reconcile units, dates, sources, formulas, contradictions, missing data, and qualifiers before arranging sections.
3. **Write the fast path.** Title, headings, decisive evidence, captions, caveat, and conclusion should communicate the argument to the least specialized named stakeholder.
4. **Preserve the audit path.** Keep exact method, technical vocabulary, assumptions, tables, and sources available without dominating the first read.
5. **Order by reader need.** Source order is not information architecture.
6. **Choose the opening.** Lead with the answer plus basis, aligned comparison, important trend, working tool, or strongest honest state—not a generic category title.
7. **Remove ceremony.** Each section answers a new question. Combine repeated summary, rationale, and conclusion sections.

## Trust and claims

- Preserve every qualifier, population, period, unit, condition, and uncertainty that changes meaning.
- Simplify language without broadening the claim.
- Distinguish measured fact, customer statement, projection, recommendation, and causation.
- Do not invent urgency, ownership, approvals, confidentiality, customer intent, future behavior, or deadlines.
- Describe the method used and limitations that affect interpretation; omit tool diaries and failed attempts unless they change confidence.
- Prefer a concrete supported statement over vague praise such as “best,” “safe,” “fast,” or “massive.”
- Put sources and methodology close enough to audit, but do not repeat them beside every derived value.

## Opening patterns

- **Decision:** answer and decisive basis are co-primary.
- **Comparison:** alternatives share a common visual basis so readers see the difference directly.
- **Trend or benchmark:** the relationship, exception, or threshold leads; exact records follow.
- **Tool:** the working calculator or configurator leads when manipulating assumptions is the job.
- **No supported conclusion:** lead with the strongest known state, implication, limit, or unresolved question.

Avoid a masthead, generic centered hero, three benefit cards, logo wall, testimonial carousel, and final CTA as an automatic sequence. Use only structures the material earns.

## Calls to action

- Match the CTA to the reader's actual state and permission. Signed-in users should not be asked to sign up.
- Use one primary next action per decision point.
- State the result of action, not a vague step: “Compare plans,” “Start deployment,” “Download benchmark.”
- Keep high-commitment actions near the evidence and terms that justify them.
- Do not use false urgency, preselected consent, hidden recurring terms, or visual hierarchy that obscures alternatives.

## Documentation

- Put the shortest successful path first, then conceptual explanation and edge cases.
- Pair code with the output or behavior it produces.
- Give code samples a copy action, language label, and accessible horizontal overflow.
- Keep commands, paths, version requirements, and expected results exact.
- Use heading hierarchy and persistent navigation for long docs; keep the current location visible.
- Provide machine-readable or Markdown access when the product supports it and the source can remain synchronized.

## Blog and changelog

- Titles state the change or argument rather than the content category.
- Include date, author/owner where relevant, scope, and affected users.
- Changelog entries explain user-visible effect, migration or compatibility needs, and links to exact docs.
- Long-form prose uses comfortable measure, stable anchors, and shareable headings.
- Provide RSS or an equivalent subscription mechanism when the site promises ongoing publication.

## Media and delight

- Use product screenshots, diagrams, customer material, or illustrations as evidence.
- Avoid stock imagery and decorative generated art that manufacture substance.
- Motion must explain continuity, demonstrate a product behavior, or confirm interaction. Do not reveal every section on scroll.
- Delight comes from clarity, subject-specific composition, and low-friction interaction—not jokes, spectacle, fake terminals, or confetti by default.

## Completion

The opening communicates the reader's job and strongest supported answer, every section advances the argument, claims retain their qualifiers, decision and audit paths agree, CTAs reflect real state, and the page was reviewed as both a narrative and a functioning interface.

## Escalate

Escalate customer names, confidentiality, pricing formulas, competitive claims, legal language, unapproved testimonials, security assertions, or recommendations whose evidence and owner are unclear.
